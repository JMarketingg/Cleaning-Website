# Service Area Landing Pages — Reference Notes

Source: Whitespark, "Guide to the Perfect Service Area Landing Page"
Saved for NeatNest House Cleaning (Makati & BGC service area business).

## What it is
A unique page per city/neighborhood we serve (vs. a location page, which is for physical storefronts). Since NeatNest comes to customers, every service area (Makati, BGC, and later expansions) should eventually get its own landing page.

## Why it matters for us
- Google Business Profile (GBP) guidelines require hiding our address if we're a service-area business (home-based or non-public office) — this often hurts local pack rankings.
- Service area landing pages fill that gap by ranking in **organic** results even when GBP visibility is limited.
- Also gets scraped by AI Overviews / ChatGPT-style answers.

## Research before writing (do this per city)
Ways to learn how customers search:
1. Surveys/polls of nearby residents
2. Competitor site analysis (e.g. Happy Helpers Manila)
3. Keyword tools (AnswerThePublic, AlsoAsked, Semrush, Moz)
4. Google "People Also Ask" + autocomplete
5. Staff/customer call notes — what words they actually use
6. Social listening (Reddit, Instagram comments)
7. AI prompting (ChatGPT/Gemini) for likely questions — verify, don't trust blindly

## Google Business Profile rules for Service Area Businesses (relevant once we set up GBP)
- Business title = real business name only, no city names appended
- One GBP per operations hub; address can be a home address but must be **hidden** in the listing
- Up to 20 service areas per listing
- Service area typically ≤ ~2 hrs driving time from base
- No separate listings per service (one listing for all cleaning services, not one per service type)
- Don't create listings at employees' addresses

## Site structure strategy
1. GBP → links to the service-area landing page for our home base city
2. That page links out to other service-area pages (Makati page links to BGC page, etc.)
3. All service area pages linked from a main "Service Areas" nav item, homepage, and/or HTML sitemap
4. Internal links from About/Contact/service pages too, so Google's crawler reaches them

## The 20 elements of a strong service area landing page
1. Logo (consistent with all branding/socials)
2. Slogan (key customer benefit, e.g. convenience)
3. Service area finder (prominent in header/hero)
4. Consistent site navigation
5. Full contact info — business name + city name together, phone/text/email/socials
6. Orientation video (optional but boosts engagement) — jobs/staff/vehicles in that city
7. Booking CTA button, repeated throughout page
8. Unique value proposition tied to that city's pain points
9. Service menu + pricing (transparent pricing filters out unqualified leads)
10. Gallery — before/after photos, staff, vehicles/uniforms for trust
11. "How it works" — process explanation + credentials/licensing/trust signals
12. Embedded map of full service radius (confirms coverage, helps referrals)
13. Reviews — specific to that city when possible (GBP, Yelp, Facebook, or first-party)
14. Review request links/icons (never incentivized — against platform rules)
15. Satisfaction guarantee
16. Community involvement for that city (sponsorships, local events, partnerships)
17. Awards/associations specific to that area
18. Related media (podcasts, local press, blog features)
19. Location-specific offers/promos
20. Final CTA at page bottom

## Advanced/optional additions
- Online booking/ordering functionality
- Dedicated Q&A section per page (don't just rely on one sitewide FAQ)
- Loyalty/referral program
- Email/text capture (lead magnet: discount, newsletter)
- Live chat
- Tools/calculators (e.g. a price estimator)
- Careers/job openings per city
- Local business cross-referral network (e.g. partner with movers, real estate agents)

## SEO specifics per page
Reflect **city name + target keywords** in:
- URL (e.g. `/cleaning-services-makati`)
- Title tag
- Meta description
- Body content
- Image file names
- Anchor text of inbound/internal links

## Duplicate content — don't worry too much
- Google rarely penalizes for shared boilerplate (e.g. same service list/pricing across city pages)
- DO vary: reviews, local details, imagery, community ties — so each page feels genuinely localized
- DON'T mass-produce thin pages with no unique value

## Practical scope decision for a small business like ours
- Don't build a page for every service × city combination (overkill)
- Instead: one set of **service area pages** (by city/neighborhood) + one set of **service description pages** (by service type), cross-linked
- Consider neighborhood-level granularity only if BGC/Makati searches show distinct sub-area intent (e.g. "cleaning services Poblacion" vs. generic "Makati")

## Maintenance
Not a set-and-forget asset — revisit when:
- Hours/services change
- New reviews come in (rotate fresh ones onto the page)
- Running new promos
- New local press/mentions to add
